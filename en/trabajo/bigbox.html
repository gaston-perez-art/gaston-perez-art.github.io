Bigbox · 2026 – today

From a gift catalog to a marketplace that learns from every user

I own Bigbox’s B2C e-commerce end to end across five LatAm countries. The challenge was twofold: the buyer is not the one who enjoys the gift, and the company had no product lifecycle.

Two users, one product

Bigbox sells experiences as gifts. The giver buys; the recipient chooses and redeems the experience. They are two different people at two different moments, and the business only saw the first one.

I defined the product vision: move from a closed catalog of boxes to an experience marketplace with offers personalized by each user’s behavior. I work on three surfaces: purchase, from discovery to checkout; redemption, with its own recipient profile; and the back office.

Repurchase: closing the loop between recipient and giver

I came in with one goal: lift repurchase from 1.4% to 2%. The recipient lived the experience and the giver never heard how it went. I built a thank-you loop: the recipient writes to the giver, the message is opened 78% of the time and brings the giver back to the site with a concrete reason to buy again. Repurchase reached 3.4%.

Conversion: from a fixed catalog to personalized offers

I led the move to a dynamic marketplace. Personalization algorithms took end-to-end conversion from 2.58% to 4.5% in the first month.

Search by date: matching supply and demand at the moment of choice

I launched activity search by date. The goal was 50% conversion; the result was 86.6%.

The recipient as a buyer

I opened a new revenue line with an upselling flow at redemption. The recipient was a user who was never monetized; now they can buy. High-margin incremental revenue, without hurting the redemption experience.

The costAny friction at redemption hits gift satisfaction. The upsell was designed to stay out of the way.

Support: resolve without an agent, without lowering satisfaction

I shipped a conversational chat on the site: it handles 8 out of 10 inquiries without reaching an agent, with a 4.7 NPS.

Setting up how we build

The company had no product lifecycle. I introduced PRDs, continuous discovery, a design process and post-release iteration, and moved a Scrum setup that no longer worked to Kanban. I set the roadmap by quarter and by half.
